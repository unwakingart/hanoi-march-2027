[ The program ]

The one-week program focuses on pencil. The second week adds charcoal and composition.

Week one: pencil

Light, shadow and form

You'll start with cubes, spheres and the space around them. This is where you learn to shade with light and shadow, measure what you see, and place a drawing well on the page.

The head and a still life

You'll learn to draw the head by breaking it into simple flat planes, then draw a classical bust. The week ends with a still life full of different textures.

Week two: charcoal and composition

Right: two pages on composition from Drawing Made Easy by E. G. Lutz, 1921.

Charcoal

Charcoal gives you deeper darks and stronger contrast than pencil. You'll work up from simple shapes to a sculpture and still-life objects, learning to handle charcoal and smudging tools and to see the big lights and darks first.

Composition

You'll study how Old Master paintings are put together, then design compositions of your own and keep improving them.

Academic approach

The classes follow an academic method, which means you start with simple forms and build up one step at a time.

  • Your teachers are academically trained Vietnamese artists, and they teach in English.
  • The course materials are inspired by the official training of the Vietnam University of Fine Arts.
  • You'll draw from real objects set up in front of you, not from photos.

Cancellation policy

  • The downpayment and installments are non-refundable, because the cohort has to run for the rest of the group.
  • You may transfer your spot to a friend. This is an all-girls cohort, so your friend has to be a girl too, and she fills in the application form as well.

  • Installments should be paid within 24 hours of the due date. If one isn't, your spot is cancelled and what you've already paid isn't refunded.
  • If I have to cancel for reasons outside my control, such as severe weather, a natural disaster, political unrest, a health emergency or travel restrictions, you get back everything you paid. Flights are your own.

[ Good to know ]

Before you apply, here are some things that you should know to see if this is the right fit for you.

You stay in a hostel dorm

  • You stay in an all-girls dorm room at a backpacker hostel, tucked down an alley in Hanoi's Old Quarter. Everyone has her own bunk. There's one bathroom in each room and more in the hallway. There are no private rooms.
  • I think this is the best way for us to really get to know each other, and it keeps the cohort affordable. We go through the whole program together, and a lot of friendships form this way.

The Old Quarter is lively

  • It's the oldest and busiest part of the city, and it really comes alive at night, with food and cafés on every street. Your evenings are free to explore it.
  • The hostel sits down an alley, which shelters it from most of the street noise. It can still be noisy at night. The hostel also has a free beer hour in the evenings.
  • The hostel and the studio are both down alleys that cars can't enter, which is normal in Hanoi, and it's how you get to see the everyday city up close. Every ride starts and ends with a short walk, so you'll want to be comfortable walking 10–15 minutes at a time on uneven streets.

Streets of Hanoi's Old Quarter.

Breakfast and lunch are arranged for you

  • On class days, breakfast is at the hostel and lunch is at a restaurant near the art studio. There's a welcome dinner on the first evening too. Meals not mentioned are not provided.
  • Because meals are arranged ahead for the whole group, I can't cater for special diets such as vegetarian or vegan. If you have a food allergy, please tell me in your application, so I know before I order.

Flights are not included

  • Flights aren't included, which leaves you free to plan the trip your way. Come a few days early or stay on afterwards to see more of Vietnam.

Do I need a visa?

  • Philippine passport holders can enter Vietnam visa-free for up to 21 days, which covers the whole trip. If you plan to arrive early or stay on, make sure your whole stay fits within that. Rules change, so please check the current rule for your passport before you book anything.

What's the weather like?

  • It's summer back home, but March in Hanoi is cool and mild, usually around 17 to 23°C. Skies are often grey, with light drizzle on many days. It's comfortable weather for walking around and for long days in the art studio.

What should I bring?

  • A light jacket or a few layers, and a small umbrella for the drizzle. Bring comfortable shoes for walking too.

How do we get to the art studio?

  • Transport to and from the art studio is included. With four or more participants, we go by private car. With fewer, we take Grab together.

What about laundry?

  • The hostel has a laundry service, charged by the kilo. At the moment it's 40,000 VND a kilo (about ₱90) for 24-hour service, or 70,000 VND a kilo (about ₱155) for 10-hour service. You pay the hostel directly.
